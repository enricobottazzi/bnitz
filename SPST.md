# Scientific Paper Structure Theory (SPST)

SPST is a BNITZ adaptation of Rhetorical Structure Theory (RST) for scientific papers. It borrows RST’s distinction between a central span (the nucleus) and attached spans, as well as its recursive structure. The nucleus is the paper’s main contribution. It can be a finding, theorem, argument, synthesis, artifact, guideline, or research plan. Each relation describes what an attached span does for its parent. [RST relation definitions](https://www.sfu.ca/rst/01intro/definitions.html)

## Six relations

Positions are evenly spaced around a circle, with 6 at the top and 1 through 5 proceeding clockwise.

| Relation | Function | Logo and position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Problem** | States the question, gap, or practical problem the parent addresses. | <img src="assets/problem-response.svg" alt="Ring with four inward leaves" width="24"> · 6 |
| **Background** | Gives prior work, definitions, or other context needed to understand the parent. | <img src="assets/circular-motif.svg" alt="Background motif" width="24"> · 1 |
| **Method** | Describes how the work was done or how it is planned. | <img src="assets/interpretation.svg" alt="Circular motif with a four-point ornament" width="24"> · 2 |
| **Implication** | States what follows from the parent, such as a prediction, an application, or a next step. | <img src="assets/implication.svg" alt="Six-petal rosette" width="24"> · 3 |
| **Evidence** | Uses results, analyses, or comparisons to support, challenge, or assess the parent. | <img src="assets/evidence.svg" alt="Evidence motif" width="24"> · 4 |
| **Scope** | Specifies where, when, or under what conditions the parent applies. | <img src="assets/explanation.svg" alt="Scope motif" width="24"> · 5 |

## Highlights

A **strong highlight** marks a planet’s main point. A **soft highlight** marks wording that helps explain or contextualize that point, such as a brief rationale or supporting detail. Both kinds of highlight belong to the same planet. Highlight only the passages that help the reader follow the argument.

## Nesting

Attach each span to the part of the argument it directly supports, explains, or qualifies. A branch can have children of its own, and its relation to the parent covers the whole branch.
